# Future acceptance: living with the forces and institutions you built

**Source:** owner's [review of PR #6](https://github.com/ferreiracade-dotcom/Caerness/pull/6), 13 October 2026.
**Status:** future narrative acceptance criterion; not a blocking feature request for
PR #6 and not evidence of implemented gameplay. Real-model access/ordinary narrative
acceptance remain the immediate gate. 3D is deferred indefinitely.

The owner enjoys building, developing and witnessing loyal forces. Distinctive
captains should command recognisable subordinate squads or elite contingents within
the overall organisation. Memories and rivalries give people identity, but the
central emotional payoff is ownership, cultivated exceptional followers, nested
hierarchy and lived institutional presence—not simply rewards for winning battles.

After a capable model is connected, include the following unscripted prose scenes
in a subsequent acceptance session:

- Observe an assembled force and its captains **before a battle**. Convey the real
  chain of command, recognisable contingents, known loyalties and readiness without
  inventing soldiers, ranks, uniforms, equipment or guaranteed allegiance.
- Spend an ordinary moment in a held stronghold. Encounter actual guards, colours
  where established, routines and people occupying the place the player built or
  secured. Enjoyment of ownership needs no bonus or new crisis.
- Watch named commanders drill their people and develop over real careers. Where
  existing fights or competition are available, include people without a Core as
  well as cultivators; do not equate cultivation with every kind of competence.
- Return to ongoing construction and witness its actual progress, limits and
  consequences. A project can matter because it is becoming part of the world,
  without a quest reward.
- Conduct a formal muster and a quiet observational scene. Assess whether the prose
  conveys personalities, hierarchy, histories and institutional presence while
  letting the player control pacing.

Verify scene claims against existing people, organisational roles/subdivisions,
relationships, troop aggregates, assets/locations, project progress and histories.
Named captains can represent aggregate contingents; this does not require simulating
each soldier individually. If those arrangements, equipment or colours are not
established, mark the missing capability or prerequisite rather than inventing them
for a flattering scene.

Record player words, model/tool choices, authoritative outcomes, prose and qualitative
weaknesses. Account for willingness, autonomy and changing circumstances. Do not
conflate a prior narrative statement with a persisted appointment, muster or resource.

Use and compose the existing general planning/domain capabilities. Do not create
bespoke "siege intro", "brand NPC" or muster command recognisers, duplicate the
simulation, force a battle, or restart the desktop task for this criterion.
