Source: `canon/world_bible_current.docx`, whole document scanned (Parts 0-3, 5-24 read in full or by targeted sweep; Part 22 underworld is covered in `2026-10-17-underworld-canon.md` and only cross-referenced here). Locators are `[N]` = paragraph index (non-empty paragraphs, document order); quotes are a few words only. Engine side read from `engine/`, `connector/` and `data/caerness.db` (opened read-only, schema 108, 900 actors of which 784 characters).

Headline: canon states social reaction as a **grammar with several independent axes** ([24], [488]-[501], [2057]); the engine stores most of the inputs (Stage, sex, age, kin, household, relationship meters, membership, religion tables) but **nothing uses Stage, rank, kinship, culture or reputation to shape how a person reacts**, and the connector scene does not even show the GM the Stage, kin tie or standing of the people present. Reactions are therefore GM habit.

## 1. Social reaction rules

### 1.1 The governing principle
- Formality is "the response to power" [24]: every society keys a codified grammar of "conduct, distance, address, deference, and precedence" to something (Churn: cultivation, lineage, martial standing; kingdoms: office and birth; Underheaven: sacred sovereignty, the most absolute pitch; looser at the Road and open ports). A breach of the grammar is itself a legible act, so entrances, deference, refusal to defer all read loudly. [23]: recognition precedes introduction; the world reacts to the announcement. [506]: the greater the standing, the less a person announces it; "Other people do the announcing."
- Presence is not a fear spell or Stage scanner [352]; the deepest masters are quieter because they contain it, so "social reaction can therefore reveal more than raw Presence." [507]: a master who suppresses current can still transform a room once recognised.
- Status is many axes, none reducible to Stage. Hearth table [488]-[501]: personal power (Stage, depth, reputation), institutional station (Head, Elder, officer, host), relationship (family, master-disciple, senior-junior, friend, rival), propriety (host/guest, setting, ceremony, challenge, mourning), reputation/character (honour, fear, infamy, reliability, cruelty), faction/local philosophy. [487]: personal power does not create office ("A wandering Fifth does not become governor"), yet is never socially irrelevant. [1485]-[1486] "Class is not Stage": poor Third in a leaking inn, Closed-Core financier with ships, Closed-Core ruler. [2057]: skill, reputation, fame, respectability, authority, wealth, taste are separate; [1911] competence, authority, wealth, celebrity, notoriety, respectability, familiarity are separate variables.

### 1.2 By Stage / cultivation
- Stage shorthand [212]-[232]: First = developing martial artist/working cultivator; Temper = professional backbone; Third = strong professional, instructor, veteran, senior disciple; Breach = "first unmistakable Master threshold"; Fifth = Great Master; Sixth = pinnacle, among strongest alive (about 40 living, 31 men and 9 women [624]-[625], [2090]).
- Social offset outside the Hearth [297]-[318]: Hearth Third = outer Temper-ish "mature strong backbone"; a Hearth Breach is filled by an outer Third; Hearth Fifth = outer Breach; Hearth Sixth = outer Fifth, and an outside Sixth is "a continental anomaly". Outside the Hearth a given Stage therefore impresses more. Counts [268]-[296]: Hearth holds 20% of Firsts, 55% of Thirds, 85% of Fifths, 95% of Sixths.
- Reaction ladder by observer, Hearth [508]: child stares until corrected; merchant becomes attentive; young First visibly impressed; seasoned Third "exactly the proper bow"; rival Breach "the minimum courtesy required"; a Fifth may only incline the head, "more than a room full of civilians standing." Who does not become formal is revealing: old friend, former master, grandmother [508], [513].
- Nobody mechanically stands for the stronger [512]; they rise for an elder, honoured guest, officeholder, famous master or significant arrival. No universal stage-specific bow angles [511]. Seating balances host, guest, age, office, relationship and martial standing, "no rule that the strongest person always receives the best chair" [512].
- Fear raises formality [514]-[516]: a recognised infamous wanted master gets exact titles, deeper bows, "painfully correct" service; civilians are not obliged to enforce bounties on people who could kill them, they comply, avoid provocation and report afterwards. Courage "does not require suicidal stupidity."
- Surpassing does not reverse a relationship: a Fifth can still bow deeply to the Third who taught them [558]; younger sibling who outranks an elder in Stage stays the younger sibling [551], [1438]; a Sixth may defer privately to an elderly parent [1438]; a Closed-Core grandmother keeps authority over a Breach grandson [1438], [513].
- Stage is not rank, office or wealth in any institution: Ashwick's Hector (110, Sixth) vs actual Head Sable (Fifth) [782]; Reedmark Head Orson (Fifth) under greatest master Jeren [770]; military: a Third can be the better general [192]. Hollow: "Stage does not equal rank" [834].
- Stage mismatch is "a fact of martial life rather than a universal ethical category" [622]; challenging down is shameful or normal "depends on context" [622], [890]. Fame is independent of Stage [1910]; a spectacular Third can be better known than a quiet Fifth [1910].
- Disability does not erase standing: Core-destroyed former Head, blind Fifth elder, one-handed physician [1560]. Scars/disfigurement do not decide narrative role [1558].
- Appearance of age: Stage shows as vitality, not preserved youth; a 60-year-old Third can look older than an 80-year-old Fifth [1581]-[1582]. Retirement is a normal life stage [1584].

### 1.3 By status, rank, office
- Authority must be observed: "Power must be acknowledged. Authority must be observed." [486]. Churn autonomy covers martial affairs only; ordinary law, taxes, magistrates apply to Churned people too [482]-[484].
- Titles keep distance: personal names imply familiarity; offices, family names, "Master", "Elder", "Head", "Teacher" keep it; "deliberately using an overly familiar form can itself be an insult" [511].
- Familiarity softens formality but station can reactivate it: a childhood friend addresses the Clan Head properly in front of the assembled House, then returns to a nickname in private [513]. Friends tease, family interrupts, an elderly woman tells a Breach grandson he is too thin [513].
- Hospitality first: in formal circumstances food, drink and rest precede business [512].
- Underheaven court [1046]-[1049]: distance is rank (Outer Capital, Ministerial City, Imperial Precinct, Inner Palace [1122]); audience is bodily (prostration, eyes lowered until addressed, prescribed distances and forms of speech); no one stands north of the throne; screen and veil as registers of access; a subject who failed gravely may kneel and offer their life in atonement [1049]. Office outweighs blood: a dismissed Averin keeps the surname but not the command [1125]. Censors are dangerous because their report may reach the emperor [1124].
- Dunmoor: a warrior who breaks an oath or abandons his dead "can lose standing without losing a single Stage" [1040]. Priesthood is an aristocratic civic career [1037], [1146]. Land, oath and inherited right frame political language [1140]; Greywood asks "by what lawful authority" [1173].
- Distinguishing office from person: Concord Head is not an eighth Seat and cannot expel, seize or compel [747]; Blackwood has no League Head, the Convener can say "the four signatory holds have agreed" [776]; Farlight mediators are trusted for procedure not truth-sensing [800].
- Respect for expertise outside the martial: Vein-Menders, referees, appraisers earn prestige without martial rank [961]-[978]; a senior steward or chief clerk may hold more practical authority than blood relatives [1489].

### 1.4 By wealth
- Wealth shows in cloth, dye, tailoring, upkeep more than cultivation Stage [562], [1505]; finery "can announce standing; it does not create it" [562]. Poor and rich of the same culture wear the same silhouette but are "instantly distinguishable" [1505].
- Wealth buys privacy, insulation, discretion and servants who do not talk [1497]-[1498]; elite patients' secrets make a physician's discretion valuable [1498]. Wealth buys assessment, travel and introductions for a gifted child [1483]; "merit matters, but the world is not perfectly meritocratic" [1483].
- Scandal costs scale with power: a wealthy man survives what ruins a dependent clerk; a famous Sixth woman can refuse conventions that bind a merchant's daughter [1451]. "Status changes the cost of violating" norms [1451].
- Dunmoor elites display land, horses, halls, retainers, feast-giving [1487]; Windmere access, ships, imports, credit; Sunreach civic property, guild standing, public works; Greywood patronage and charity; Thornward fortified houses and service; Stormhollow mines and foundries [1487]. Prestige economy: wealth converts to durable public objects [1843].
- Poverty is density and exposure (shared rooms, one warm room, no privacy) [1493], [1497], [1503]; the poor perform their own domestic labour [1490].
- Lending: debt is leverage and "evidence of distress" [925]; gambling debt turns into information leak [1655] (see underworld note).

### 1.5 By reputation and information
- Reputation is a separate record with "source, geography, confidence and audience" [1974]; hidden truth must never become omniscient consensus [1974]. Three bands: local knowledge, network knowledge, general fame [1976]. Corrections travel slower than accusations; reputation "should decay, harden, migrate and split rather than reset" [1978]. Epithets are earned by something visible, not issued at a Stage [1973].
- Persons have several dimensions that can be traded off: reputation/character axis includes honour, fear, infamy, reliability, generosity, cruelty [499].
- Information has geography [1926]; servants' knowledge, tavern talk, family news are the "private/local" channel [1949]; rumour can be seeded to hide a truth [1969].
- Underworld fame is deterrence versus operational risk [1995]; Closed-Core fighters carry working-class celebrity without Churn standing [1959], [1997]-[1998].
- Mourning wrongly (too little for near kin, too much for distant) is "a real failure legible to everyone" [1071].

### 1.6 By gender and age
- Age has dignity independent of martial ability [551]; filial respect continues into adulthood but "adulthood genuinely changes the practical relationship" [551]. Elderly relatives hold standing with Closed Cores and no martial experience [551].
- Churn martial society is male-majority (70-75% men; summit more skewed) with no hard female ceiling and no universal male succession law [624]-[626]; exceptional women are normal enough that a female Sixth is not "impossible" [625]. Coven mantle succession is male-only [831] (the only canon male-only rule inside the Churn world).
- Sexual double standard: in conservative households a woman's reputation weighs more in marriage negotiation, "a social double standard rather than a biological law" [1451]; Greywood puts stronger moral pressure on marriage and fidelity than Saltmere nightlife [1450]; erotic art is more private in Hearth elite culture, openly bought in Windmere, pressured in Greywood, varies by Sunreach charter city [1908].
- Power-based coercion (master over junior disciple, heir over servants or tenants, patron over dependant) is plausible and recurring; "The governing principle is power rather than a simple moral division between the sexes" [1452]-[1453]. Some Houses shield important sons until scandal is unburiable, others punish predatory disciples severely [1453]. Women use shared housing, trusted seniors, guards, reputations that make retaliation costly [1453].
- Female celebrity treated variably (technical analysis, obsession with clothing, romanticisation) [1914]-[1916]; handsome male champions are also sexualised [1915].
- Pregnancy: no loss of Stage; combat gets dangerous; midwifery respected [1572]-[1574]. Higher-stage men remain fertile longer, so half-siblings decades apart occur [1577].
- Thornward/Dunmoor/Greywood/Stormhollow succession is male-preferring (see 3); Windmere daughters inherit before distant male collaterals [1154]; Underheaven throne male only, empress can dominate a regency [1120].

### 1.7 Strangers versus known people, Churned versus Hearth, outsiders
- Strangers: a shopkeeper may not recognise an unknown visitor but reads the room [507]; a foreign martial artist may misread titles, seating, insignia [565].
- Veinborn / cultivator / martial artist / Churned / Hearthfolk are five independent labels [475]-[476]. The Churn is "not portable" [477]: a Dunmoor martial family is not Churned. Churned and non-Churned Hearthfolk share one civilisation of "Age, family, formality, hospitality, reputation, face, dress, hierarchy, and acknowledgement of standing" [478]; the Churn adds master-disciple relations, martial seniority, lineage protocol, challenge culture, Convergence and Road customs [478].
- Hearth localism is cultural not racial; outsiders marked by accent, dress, food, etiquette, knowledge of local institutions [565]. A foreign-born Churned person remains "one of us, but not from here"; sharpest suspicion is the specific fear that a foreign disciple will carry the deepest inheritance out [566], [479]. Social acceptance and transmission access are separate [479], [812] (Redmarsh needs sponsor, years, loyalty, household incorporation).
- Hearthfolk civilians know famous Houses and masters, follow the Convergence, argue about fighters [568]; martial reputation is public culture, not a niche.
- Hollow: universally reviled across the Churn ("a hardened Undertow warlord and a devout Vigil priest agree") [820]; membership alone makes one Hollow in Churn terms [542]-[544]; leaving does not erase the label [544]. A Hollow cannot be a Witness in good standing [1062].
- The Witness in pagan kingdoms: refusing public oaths and sacrifices causes friction (Dunmoor reading of signs [1035]-[1036]; Thornward [1097]; Sunreach Common Flame oaths [1206]); Underheaven accepts Witness oaths by the Creator [1052]; Windmere tolerates everything [1087]; Stormhollow's Keeping does not demand worship so less friction [1236], [1101].
- Foreign travel shock: bedding, meal hours, bathing, room layout, footwear, table manners, servant etiquette [1517].
- Translation misreads institutions ("ruler of the martial houses" for the Concord Head) [1981].

### 1.8 Courtesy, bowing, insult, face
- Bow is "ordinary social grammar, not a stage calculator"; depth, timing, whether returned, who initiates communicate relationships [511]. "The bow substitutes for the contest." [510]. "Same bows. Different room." [502]. Formality "is a shared grammar, not proof of admiration" [503], [515].
- Insult forms: overfamiliar address [511]; minimum courtesy [508]; refusing to defer [24]; mocking a House in thinly disguised theatre [1900]; scandal sheets [1961]; epithets that stick [1973]; rejection of a private challenge read as cowardice (Rookwood ignores such accusations [804]); satire can permanently attach an insult to a magistrate [1991].
- Face and reputation of the household: conduct "reflects upon the household" but "a distant cousin's crime" does not automatically destroy kin [551]. Wrong mourning is a legible failure [1071]. Desecrating ancestral graves is "exceptionally serious"; returning an enemy's body can signal respect without regret [560], [549]; Dunmoor: deliberate desecration "far more than an insult" [1041].
- Refusing a challenge: Long Road credibility comes from answering only the fights one means to answer, refusal is not itself cowardice [854]; no right to compel a fight [853]; challenge culture varies by Current (see 4) [608]-[622].
- Death etiquette: lawful challenge deaths are not murder in Concord [549]; indifference is possible, desecration is something stronger [549].

### 1.9 Deference and its limits
- Deference is conditional and layered: state law, House proprietary authority and Churn custom overlap [484]; "Conflicts between those layers are a feature" [484].
- Fear is not respect [515]-[516]; hatred can hide in propriety [515]. Ordinary people owe no heroism to bounties [516].
- Concord Head limits [747]; Seat votes [745]; emperor's mandate is conditional and unjust killing is how a mandate slips [1049]; Inheritor's reverence rests on power fused with inheritance and weakens with power [1056]; Law of the Current stands above the Inheritor [1057].
- Kinship does not equal benevolence: "parents can be cruel, spouses abusive, elders corrupt" [1438].
- Rival, inferior, superior in the Hearth: rival Breach gives minimum courtesy [508]; inferior (junior disciple, servant) owes respect with loyalty "without becoming the master's property" [558]; superior (master) owes "serious instruction, correction, proper transmission, and reasonable responsibility" [558]. A master can pressure a junior by controlling access to teaching [1452].
- Institutions respond differently to fear and breakdown: one master treats fear as ordinary, another turns breakdown into shame [1570].

## 2. Household, family and House culture

### 2.1 What a household is
- "The household is the basic social unit, but not a universal template" [1436]. It decides who feeds you, who cares for you when ill, who inherits, whose reputation your conduct touches, which elders have claims on your loyalty [1437]. Forms: couple and children, three-generation farm family, merchant family with clerks and servants under one roof, Dunmoor manor full of retainers, Windmere trading house with apprentices, Churn Great House compound, free-company winter household, widowed parent with adult children nearby, religious or professional community [1437].
- Households are economic units [1439]: land, shops, tools, boats, livestock, contracts, manuals, dowries, debts and care obligations move through them; richer ones have servants, clerks, retainers, nurses, guards, grooms, stewards.
- Hearth: "You belong to people before you belong only to yourself", strongly familial "without making the household an absolute command structure" [551]. A Great House is also an institution, making "succession, inheritance, marriage, adoption, disowning, and transmission matters of public consequence" [552].

### 2.2 Head's authority and its limits
- Parents hold substantial authority over children; adulthood changes it [551]. Authority remains strong in adolescence while the young person acquires an identity through work, training, marriage prospects [1457].
- Head's powers are institution-specific: Rannick Head controls external relations, senior appointments, deepest inheritance, but branches with "six generations of estates" have real weight, "not a court with everyone beneath Voss personally" [761]; Stonewatch Head controls diplomacy, land, deepest transmission and martial obligations, affiliated families keep property and internal seniority [797]; Blackwood has no head, a Convener [776]; Nine Ferries has three separate roles (martial master, chief speaker, major student) [779]; Kellbrook Head is recognised by teaching authority though Kerr kin are everywhere [785]; Redmarsh is closed by difficulty of incorporation not blood [812].
- Bad heads are real: cruel households humiliate Closed-Core children; healthy ones redirect talent [1468]. Abuse by powerful members of dependants is a plausible crime, handled by ordinary criminal law [1452].

### 2.3 Obligations both ways
- Down: feed, house, teach, protect, arrange marriage, pay for assessment/training where affordable [1465]; masters owe instruction and responsibility [558]; Great Houses give a Closed-Core relative a central role as administrator, accountant, diplomat [1467]; a sect child remains someone's child; letters, visits, support continue [1480].
- Up: filial respect, care in illness and age, mourning by exact kinship [1071], tending the dead [1070], keeping the line (a line without heirs leaves its dead untended, giving "religious weight to the pressure a family already feels") [1072]; disciple's loyalty "appropriate to the relationship" [558]; conduct reflecting on the House [551].
- Poor household choice: accept a respected institution's offer for a promising child (food, status, future) versus keeping essential labour or protecting the child [1465].

### 2.4 Kin, heirs, spouses, retainers, servants, stewards, dependants
- Kin: descent, marriage, adoption, household incorporation and chosen obligations [1441]; adoption is "socially real rather than merely charitable", creating inheritance and filial duty, though great martial lineages may separate ordinary adoption from restricted transmission access [1441]; Churn kinship has unusual visible institutional weight: bloodline, marriage, collateral branches, adopted members, retainers, affiliated households [1442]. A clan is asked "Whose family is this?", a sect "Whose teaching is this?" [517]-[518].
- Spouses: marriage joins people and usually households [1445]; a spouse does not take on the other's institution automatically (a Concord woman marrying an Undertow man stays Concord unless incorporated) [1448]; foreign spouse is not culturally Hearth-born overnight [1448]. Mixed-lifespan marriages are ordinary; high-stage person outlives spouse by decades, affecting inheritance, remarriage and care [1586]-[1587].
- Heirs: no simple eldest-child rule at Rannick (capability, branch acceptance, martial inheritance, household politics); Barrow's departure made succession "permanently sensitive" [761]; Kastel lacks primogeniture, does not advertise succession [766]; Stonewatch favours someone embedded but not primogeniture or strongest [797]; Hollow Inheritor selects, favours, teaches, watches whether the powerful follow; heirs "contestable" [1064], [834]; Kellbrook/Reedmark Heads emerge through senior-master recognition, blood relatives have no automatic claim [770], [785].
- Retainers: Rannick holds retainer families, sworn household disciples, estate officials, dependent households [761]; Dunmoor retainer networks [1597]; Underheaven service families, military retainers [1442]; hereditary retainer families vs wage workers vs live-in vs daily [1489]; guard families are long-serving [937]. Important people are protected by relatives, retainers and household guards, not concealment arts [937].
- Servants and stewards: [1488]-[1491]. A senior steward or chief clerk may exercise more practical authority than many blood relatives [1489]; servants know things (the "Hush" and servants' knowledge channel [1949], [1730] in underworld note). Servants who "do not talk" are a purchased good [1498]. No continent-wide chattel slavery; unfreedom arises through wage labour, tenancy, hereditary service, indenture, debt bondage, prison and military obligation, household dependence and criminal coercion [1491]. Dependent staff may be pressured by heirs who know "refusal may threaten their family's position" [1452].
- Dependants: the household has "dependants, work, bodies, budgets" [1435]; widow's sale of a spouse's tools is a major financial decision [1519].

### 2.5 Inheritance and succession norms
- Inheritance travels through the household: land, shops, tools, contracts, manuals, dowries [1439]; objects are kept and inherited [1518]; Dunmoor heirlooms stay in a house for centuries [953], [1877].
- Churn inheritance: House succession is a martial matter under Churn custom [483]. Surname, residence, succession, which arts may cross a marriage, obligations to the birth family and children's status are negotiated in lineage marriages; Sworn Consort Accords [555], [1446]. Patrilineal or matrilineal clans possible, no universal male law [626].
- Closed-Core heirs: supportive redirection to administration, quiet disappointment, or cruelty depending on the House [555], [1467]-[1468].
- States: Underheaven imperial designation, male dynast [1120]; Dunmoor strongly agnatic, daughters transmit claims, queen regnant means near-collapse [1138]; Greywood male-line, male-preferred, daughter only when male continuation failed [1175]; Windmere male-preference but daughters before distant male collaterals [1154]; Stormhollow male-preference primogeniture [1230]; Thornward elective, adult son starts ahead with no legal right [1217]; Sunreach no crown, First Convenor chosen for a term [1200].
- Household succession elsewhere: Thistlewood's ugly succession dispute drove two branches out [806]; Reedmark/Rannick/Kastel as above.

### 2.6 Marriage
- No single continental model [1445]. Ordinary people: emotional and practical; wealthy and martial: land, alliances, inheritance, transmission, reputation [1445]. Open-Core heredity affects desirability but spouses are not breeding stock [555], [1443]. Hearth families know which lineages produce strong Cores [555].
- Regional frames [1447]: Dunmoor estates, retainer loyalties, horse lines; Windmere port charters, shipping, credit; Greywood Witness teaching plus property and dynastic interest; Sunreach through city, guild and commercial networks; Thornward and Stormhollow regional and landed obligations.
- Adultery, unmarried lovers, secondary households, mistresses, same-sex relationships, celibacy all occur, judged differently by religion and region [1450].
- Undertow ties by marriage restrain violence (disciples marry into outside families) [756]; Rannick-Kastel intermarriage [768]; Greywood-Dunmoor old marriages [143].

### 2.7 Loyalty, disloyalty, grievances, leaving service
- Loyalty is a relationship not a membership: no Road membership ceremony [839]; Waystone trust is reputational and lost by betraying guests, selling whereabouts, stealing deposits [844].
- Repudiating or being cast out of a true discipleship "is culturally serious because the relationship was more than lessons" [558]; multiple true masters are "socially more complicated" [558]; Barrow left Rannick and became an independent Sixth: "institutional wound" [761].
- Breaking contracts or settlements: partners demand sureties, hostages, advance payment, neutral witnesses, stronger escorts or refuse to contract [756].
- Leaving a coven does not erase the label [544]; Coven code protects members from unsanctioned predation [1057]; a failed member may offer their life to the Inheritor in atonement [1062].
- Abuse and its aftermath: institutions' responses differ; protection or punishment [1453]. Trauma has aftermath: veteran hypervigilance, survivor fear of spaces, master withdrawing after losing a disciple [1570].
- Retirement is normal, not defeat [1584]; Thistlewood's decline came from succession dispute plus branch departure [806].
- Grievances travel as songs and satire [1991]; Greywood-Dunmoor each hold incompatible claim archives [1185].

## 4. Institutions' internal culture

- **Master-disciple (sects, schools)** [556]-[558], [1475]-[1480]: teachers vs personal master; discipleship is selective, enduring, and carries correction and permanent elder status; chores are part of communal function not cruelty [1479]; a local Third-stage teacher shapes society ("Thirds train society") [1475]; a master can pressure juniors by controlling teaching [1452]; Redmarsh needs sponsor and years [812]; Rookwood keeps few obligations outside itself [803].
- **Concord** [718]-[749]: five Seats quorum, four for ordinary action, five for binding, six for foundational change; Head cannot expel, declare war, seize land; Guard not an army; regional offices at Highford, Stonecross, Waymeet, Eastwatch [1360]. Members are proud and internally rivalrous (White Laurel vs Red Cedar) [737], [749].
- **Undertow clans/sects/leagues** [752]-[788]: no code; personal guarantee, truce, surety, neutral host, mediator [756]; Nine Ferries a common neutral host [780]; Blackwood convener not head [776]; warlord = personal power and sworn loyalty [788].
- **Hollow** [818]-[834], [1054]-[1066]: Inheritor (male, one living, no retirement), Deep Court, Hand, Hall Lords, Orders; Law of the Current above all; protection of members until a sanctioned ordeal.
- **Guilds and professions**: reputation, apprenticeship, patronage count more than licences [961]; Still Hand neutral procurement guild [955]; Counting House severe last-resort lender with Ledger Guard [920]-[925]; referees professional [976]; Windmere charter guild privileges [1157]; Sunreach guild standing [1603].
- **Armies and companies**: Stage is not rank [192]; Crown Wardens trained for war not reputation [1129]; Palace Guard first duty is Crownhold [1131]; Western orders (Pilgrim Shield, Order of Marek vow discipline [693], Oakspire, War College [1182]); free/chartered companies preserve techniques because they worked [706]; companies have comradeship rather than lineage [1442]; escort companies are businesses with reputations [928]-[937]; Blackwood retainers fight in small organised groups [777]; Thornward prizes the officer who holds a line [1220]; Stormhollow prizes the hill-fighter and lord who holds a pass [1233].
- **Churches and temples**: Witness pastor-bishop-archbishop line; polity is a human arrangement [1015]-[1021]; Dunmoor priesthood is aristocratic civic office with High College and Readers [1037]-[1039]; Underheaven rites are state duty [1050]; Dayfire Flamekeepers have no high priest [1091]; Tidefaith figures local and various [1086]; Keeping of Names Keepers settle lineage disputes [1100].
- **Prize and Closed-Core fight world, underworld**: see underworld note.
- **Waystones**: local peace upheld by reputational cost, not rule [844]-[845].
- **Households as institutions of service**: stewards and chief clerks hold practical authority [1489]; hereditary retainer families vs wage workers [1489].

## 5. What the engine already has

### 5.1 Modules and tables (checked)
- `relationships` (3288 rows in the packaged DB): per-directed-pair `stance, familiarity, trust, affection, respect, fear, loyalty, resentment, obligation, professional_confidence`. `engine/relationships.py` provides `relationship_view`, `adjust_relationship(_pair)`, `record_relationship_effect` with `relationship_history` (0 rows at opening). Only 572 of the 3288 rows coincide with a kin link; the rest come from teaching, membership and other seeding.
- `engine/interactions.py::_social_response` (line 353): a recipient's accept/refuse score is built from a base per interaction kind (conversation .9 ... threat -.9), relationship `trust/55 + stance/75 + obligation/90`, drives `pragmatism .55, loyalty .25, risk_tolerance x stakes, aggression` (demand/threat), terms `incentive/burden/coercion`, and a small random term. After it resolves it writes relationship deltas (fear, resentment, trust, respect).
- `actor_drives` (320 rows): aggression, risk_tolerance, patience, loyalty, ambition, pragmatism. Only for some characters (784 characters in the DB).
- `engine/reputation.py` + `social_reputation` (0 rows at opening): subject_kind/subject_id/domain/value 0-100/metadata. `value()` reads by weighted domain; `adjust()` called from `education.py`, `patronage.py`, `religion.py` (domain `religious_reliability`); `martial_instruction_public()` used by `seeking.py`. Nothing in connector or `_social_response` reads it.
- `engine/household_life.py`: `households` (263; wealth, liquidity, staff_capacity, cohesion, hospitality), `household_members` (543 rows: roles only `partner` 267, `head` 267, `member` 9; `authority` 0-100, `dependency`), `household_unions`, `kinship_links` (spouse 534, parent 17, child 17, sibling 4), `guardianships`, `household_dependencies` (0 rows), `household_service_terms` (0 rows), `household_succession_plans` (0 rows). `_resolve_service_terms` (line 947): yearly review; score = loyalty*.3 + satisfaction*.3 + renewal_bias*.2 + household cohesion*.2, minus 20 if household declining; leaves service below 45 and ends the membership with a life event. `add_service_term` is the only creator (steward/retainer get authority 35, others 15). Nothing raises or lowers loyalty/satisfaction after creation; wages, treatment and conduct do not feed it.
- `engine/civilian_life.py`: `household_succession_plans` with heir, method, certainty, dispute_pressure (0 rows at opening).
- `engine/psych.py`: dependency, stressor, support, bereavement pressures (health-side), not social behaviour.
- `engine/motives.py::derive_actor_motives`: reads health, household (head => continuity goal; cohesion; liquidity), enterprise, religious obligations, travel, education, political office and claims. Culturally neutral.
- `engine/religion.py` + tables: `religious_traditions` (10 seeded: witness, hearth_rites, vigil, imperial_observance, dunmoor_pantheon, hollow_faith, tidefaith, dayfire, thornward_old_gods, keeping_of_names), `religious_affiliations` (0 rows), offices, observances, obligations (all 0), `religion_context()`/`cultural_context()` for narration. Reputation penalty for missed obligations exists, but no person carries a faith at opening.
- `engine/politics.py`: polity, dynasty, succession claims, territorial control; 7 polities, dynasty members 0, claims 0. Ruler succession exists; it is state-level, not household-level.
- `engine/embodiment.py`: `build_embodiment_packet` hands the GM coarse relationship bands (`stance_band`, `trust_band`, `has_obligation`) for present people and rendering rules ("Render speech through the protagonist's established voice, culture, status, competence"). It drops respect, fear, loyalty, kin, Stage, rank. `character_embodiment` has 0 rows.
- `institution_memberships` (324, all `membership_kind='member'`, free-text `role_text`), `teaching_relationships` (54), `typed_relationship_facts` (78; direct_transmission 50, parent_of 17, spouse 3, teacher_of 4 ...), `underworld_personnel` (49; role_kind boss/crew_leader, `authority`).
- Stage: `cultivation_profiles.current_stage` (784 rows; 201 with no Stage, 138 First, 124 Temper, 116 Third, 78 Breach, 87 Fifth, 40 Sixth) plus `core_state`. Demographic sex and birth day: `actor_life_profiles`. `background_person_profiles` (2272) also holds `social_archetype`, `current_stage`.

No age band, Stage, kin tie to the protagonist, relationship, rank, wealth or reputation. `lookup()` for a person adds `basics`, `known` knowledge lines and, if a relationship row exists, rounded meters (`stance, familiarity, trust, ... respect, fear, loyalty`) that the rulebook then forbids showing as numbers.

## 6. Gaps

### 6.1 Where canon gives rules the engine neither stores nor uses
1. **Reaction by Stage with regional offset** [297]-[318]: stored Stage is not surfaced; no outer-world offset; the GM cannot know that a Hearth Third is a notable local master in Windmere but ordinary in Cairnholt.
2. **Recognition** [23], [507]: no per-observer model of who recognises whom (fame by geography/audience [1974], [1976]); `present` does not say who knows the protagonist.
3. **Churned / Hearthfolk / Veinborn / foreigner standing** [475]-[479], [565]-[566]: no field; Current position (Concord, Undertow, Unsworn, Hollow, Long Road) is derivable only through institutions, not stored per person ([521]-[522] say it applies only inside the Churn).
4. **Culture of origin** as a social input (accent, etiquette literacy, outsider friction): derive from `home_location_id` -> polity, but no stored origin vs current location, no acculturation, no languages (canon: language map open [5951]-[5953]).
5. **Seniority / age / kin precedence** [551], [558]: stored birth day and kin links; not combined into who defers to whom. The scene says "spouse of X" but not "your uncle", "your steward", "your elder brother".
6. **Face, insult and formality of address** [511]-[516]: purely narrative; no state beyond meters.
7. **Household roles** [1488]-[1491]: packaged DB has only head/partner/member; no steward, clerk, retainer, nurse, groom, guard, tutor, apprentice rows; `household_dependencies` and `household_service_terms` empty, so a protagonist's own House has no seeded servants for the GM to rely on.
8. **Head authority limits and branch weight** [761], [797]: `authority` is a bare number; nothing about branches, retainer families, or who can overrule the head.
9. **Succession norms** [1120], [1138], [1154], [1175], [1217], [1230], [761], [766]: polity `succession_method` exists; household and institution succession rules (primogeniture vs capability vs recognition vs elective) are not parameterised per culture in households.
10. **Loyalty dynamics** [558], [756]: service loyalty is static; no events change it; sureties, hostages and guarantees [756] exist as agreements at best.
11. **Marriage as inter-lineage bargain** [555], [1446]: Sworn Consort Accords, name/residence/arts negotiation not stored.
12. **Mourning grades and ancestral rites** [1071], [1070]: `hearth_rites` tradition exists but no affiliations, no mourning state, no ancestral hall obligation rows.
13. **Sexual double standard and coercion by power disparity** [1451]-[1453]: no social-risk input in marriage or reputation; sexual coercion aftermath is mentioned as required elsewhere (underworld note) but there is no consequence engine here.
14. **Challenge etiquette by Current** [608]-[622]: Current not stored; tournament module separate.
15. **Waystone peace and local truce** [844]-[845]: locations marked as Waystones exist (The Waystone, Waymeet) but no peace rule attached to behaviour.
16. **Public reputation separate from competence** [1974]: `social_reputation` has the right shape (domain, subject) but zero rows and no audience/geography/confidence fields beyond `metadata_json`.

### 6.2 What the connector hands the GM (cheapest levers)
- The scene gives sex, a role string and organisations. It omits: Stage band (as "a notable master" language, not a number), apparent age band, kin tie to the protagonist (derivable from `kinship_links`), the relationship's qualitative band (embodiment already computes `stance_band`/`trust_band`), whether the person recognises the protagonist, the person's household role (head/steward/retainer/servant of whose House), the polity/region they belong to, and the faith. All inputs exist in the DB except recognition, faith and culture-of-origin.
- `lore` is query-driven and capped at 3 passages; the etiquette grammar [24], [488]-[516] and regional manners (Part 21 [1588]-[1607]) would need to be asked for by the GM, which is how reactions drift into habit.

### 6.3 Canon leaves open or provisional
- Exact language/dialect map [5951]-[5953]; detailed censorship law [6037]; full festival calendars [6035]-[6036]; exact household forms, feast foods, shrine conventions [5991]; civil law, wages, price bands [5938]-[5949]; Part 21 status says "some regional civil-law rules" remain open [1611].
- Whether "Churned" is the final demonym [5890]-[5891]; whether non-martial cultivators use the Six Stages [5887]-[5888].
- Independent belts and minor polities "working sketch" [144]-[145]; Empire macroregion manners not described [107]-[110]; Greywood capital placement [1185].
- No canon wage scale or service-contract rules for servants or retainers beyond "wage, tenancy, hereditary service, indenture, debt bondage" [1491]; no rule for a steward's formal duties; no explicit canon on how servants address a head (only that formality scales with station and that Underheaven is most formal).
- Specific forms of address by region are not given (only the Hearth titles [511] and the court forms [1046]); bowing norms outside the Hearth and Empire are not stated (Dunmoor, Greywood, Windmere, Sunreach, Thornward, Stormhollow only get the general "kingdoms key formality to office and birth" [24]).
- Thresholds and numbers for reactions are deliberately absent: "There are no universal stage-specific angles" [511]; the bible states reactions as ranges of human behaviour, so any engine mapping is a design choice, not canon.
- [1610] explicitly says household grief, disappointment, whether a marriage is loving, how a worker feels about a patron "belong primarily to narrative interpretation grounded in persistent facts": canon wants the deterministic layer to remember facts (kin, office, debt, injury, ownership) and the narration to humanise them.
