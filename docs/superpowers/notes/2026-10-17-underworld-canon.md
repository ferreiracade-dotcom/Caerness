Date: 2026-10-17. Source: `canon/world_bible_current.docx`, whole document scanned. Locators are `[N]` = paragraph index (non-empty paragraphs, document order) plus opening words. Part Twenty-Two (paras 1615-1840) is the main source; other hits are noted. DB read read-only (`data/caerness.db`).

## 1. General rules the canon sets

- Structure. [1617] "The underworld is not one hidden kingdom": no thieves' guild, no universal crime lord, no single gambling syndicate, no fifth Current of criminals. It is overlapping markets and relationships; one person can sit in several networks without any controlling the others. [1618] "The basic structural rule": an ecosystem of dependencies, not a pyramid; durable power comes from bottlenecks, repeat relationships, property, reputation, access and succession, not from controlling every illegal activity in a city. Restated in [6121] (no universal assassin guild, no one criminal pyramid).
- Legality is separate from respectability. [1619]-[1642] "Legality, respectability, and criminality are separate axes": gaming, sex work, prizefighting, lending, shipping and information each have a legal/tolerated form and a coercive form, and one establishment can hold both (legal gaming house with an illegal high-stakes room; legitimate brothel with one trafficking manager; respectable prize hall renting its basement to unlicensed promoters). [5949] repeats that legality varies by conduct and jurisdiction.
- Nightlife is ordinary economy. [1644] "Nightlife is not shorthand for brothels": most workers are ordinary; crime parasitizes cash, privacy, intoxication, anonymity. Classes mix without becoming equal ([1646]). Privacy is a purchased prestige good ([1649]).
- Gambling. [1652]-[1655]: wagers on cards, dice, races, fights, tournaments; stakes can be goods and rights; irreplaceable lineage items almost never casual stakes. [1653] "Serious bookmakers are reputation businesses": cheating winners loses the room; paying winning tickets is near sacred; they can be ruthless collecting debts. Addiction becomes leverage: a merchant gambles, borrows from a criminal lender, then leaks commercial information ([1655]). [897] "Gambling is ordinary tournament economy": betting does not make a contest criminal; coercion, fraud, bribery and illicit debt enforcement do. [1960]-[1961] odds sheets are public language.
- Fight fixing. [1660] "Fight fixing is more sophisticated": buying information, odds manipulation, threats to relatives, compromising trainers, hiding injury, referees, delaying travel, drugging, or asking a fighter only to survive a set time; fighters who value honour over money are expensive.
- Prizefighting. [1662]-[1693]: Closed-Core fighting is a real profession (prize halls, back-room rings, pits, private clubs, sealed cultivator bouts); careers short, injury heavy. [1684] "Closed-Core fighters are easier for criminal promoters to control" and economically vulnerable; the debt fighter is the dark pathway ([1685]: debt assigned to a promoter, fees added so it never declines). Not every pit fighter is coerced ([1686]). Women fight women; the market can exploit them but is not uniform ([1688]-[1690]). Unsanctioned cultivated bouts exist but are disdained by mainstream tournaments ([1692]-[1693]).
- Commercial sex. [1695]-[1697]: ordinary prostitution and courtesan culture overlap but differ; brothels are not automatically criminal; three recurring economic forms (owner/worker-managed; patronage-heavy with debt and dependence; criminal and coercive), and ownership change can move a house between forms over a generation. Women own houses, lead gangs, fence, run books, command enforcers, traffic people ([1701]).
- Trafficking. [1704] "Trafficking is distinct from consensual adult sex work": kidnapped, coerced and debt-trapped people; bandits sell captives into networks. [1705] legitimate house owners usually oppose traffickers (principle or business); [1706] workers keep informal defences (blacklists, doormen, physicians, check-ins). Sexual coercion by power disparity is structural ([1698]-[1702]) and must create aftermath.
- Information. [1707] "The nightlife information world" table: Painted Veil (salons, courtesan houses, gaming rooms), Hush (servants, dockhands, cooks, porters), Red Board (fighters, trainers, bettors; information becomes odds), independent brokers (resell fragments), Mallow cells (information used for physical access to cultivators). [1750] "Access brokers are especially important in Cairnholt and Crownhold": they know which clerk, servant, official, inspector, physician or household can be bribed or pressured; they look respectable and rarely commit visible violence. [1971]-[1973] intelligence is laundered into public rumour via minor writers.
- Assassination. [1734] "Professional killing occupies its own market": the Reckoning at the summit (reliable, secret, absolute prohibitions incl. never targeting children, members obscure); below it lesser networks, freelances, gang enforcers and the Cormorants ([1735]); states have sanctioned killers, the criminal market is killing purchased privately ([1736]). The Painted Veil is explicitly not an assassination service and may arrange removal only in exceptional cases ([1728]).
- Durable vs street gangs. [1738] "Street gangs rise and disappear quickly. Durable criminal power looks different": owns property, keeps legitimate fronts, pays people regularly, manages succession, accumulates favours, cultivates officials, controls a repeat bottleneck; over generations it grows family branches, marriages, trusted outsiders, archives, debts and succession disputes. [1739] "A martial crime family may train household fighters": no special crime martial art needed; stage is one power among several (a Closed-Core accountant can outrank a Third-stage nephew). [1820] durable groups often begin legitimate (discharged soldiers, road wardens, timber crews, carriers, protection companies; deserters becoming a hereditary smuggling family in two generations). [1836] "Underworld institutions should persist, reproduce and mutate": owners die, children inherit, partners split, a gang becomes a family, fronts turn criminal or respectable; [1838] underworld life continues off-camera and is downstream of economics and politics, not a separate spawning system.
- Pay and loyalty of members. Canon says only that durable groups "pay people regularly" ([1738]); there is no wage scale, oath or blood-tie rule, or rank titles for criminal organisations. Greyhook's most valuable people are clerks, foremen, boat captains, warehouse managers, customs contacts ([1746]). Mallow has layers: inner lineage, hidden support layer, public gray/legit employment, wider helpers who know fragments ([1758]). Loyalty mechanisms mentioned: reputation, repeat dealing, kinship, inevitability, not formal rolls. The Hush has no roll, head or HQ ([1731]). Canon leaves exact payment structure open.
- Violence norms. [1743] Tallow "preferred violence is controlled": murdered debtors and smashed shops are bad economics; reputation, inevitability and selective examples work better; younger branches that mistake visible brutality for strength cause internal conflict. [1746] Greyhook: violence is a contingency when paperwork and relationships fail. [1784] Cairnholt: flamboyant street crime is poor strategy. [1775] reputation substitutes for law; predictable cooperation is often more profitable than betrayal; violence is a backstop. [1816] Sunreach criminals look like commercial machines.
- Retaliation and disputes between outfits. [1774] "Criminals need agreements too, but there is no thieves' court": a family handles its own members; two bookmakers use a trusted intermediary; smugglers settle by whoever controls the warehouse or ship; pleasure-house owners mediate among establishments; Farlight can arbitrate a narrow question. No canon rule for gang-war mechanics; Mallow vs Painted Veil is clandestine, not open gang war ([1764]). Stated relations: services traded without control (Greyhook with Cormorants, fences, independents [1746]).
- Law and watch. [1770]-[1772] "Ordinary criminal law remains the responsibility of states and cities": layered enforcement (watches for violence, investigators for repeat offenders, revenue/customs for smuggling, cultivated officers for targets watches cannot arrest); a Third-stage boss with twenty followers is handled by isolating money, arresting weaker associates, seizing legal assets, informants. Corruption varies (coin, family, debt, belief that a family keeps order, completely clean). A city can know a family exists and tolerate it until a new magistrate, rising violence, lost protection or a crossed line. [1778] most gangs, bookmakers, smugglers and brothel owners are not Current-classified; an unaffiliated cultivated crime family could be Unsworn.
- Smuggling and fencing. [1748] "Fencing is a profession": stolen goods need provenance broken; Aldenmoor for manuscripts and provenance, Saltmere international redistribution, Toll's Reach physical movement, Aldergate paperwork and warehousing. [1830] cross-border chains of specialists, no continental mafia; reputation loses resolution across borders ([1831]). [946] no Elixir Syndicate; Toll's Reach gateway/smuggling port, Saltmere laundering and redistribution, Aldergate invoices/warehouses/credit, Stonegate and Spine routes, Starfall islands as transshipment.
- Debt. [1749] "Predatory lenders operate beneath and beside respectable credit": they lend to gamblers, small merchants, sex workers, laborers, pit fighters, junior martial artists; some prefer unrepayable loans because the desired asset is labour, ownership, access or information. Legal vs illegal lenders distinguished ([1620], [1635]). Stormhollow debt is company-store labour debt, structurally different from Aldergate gambling debt ([1824]). The Counting House [921-924] is the legal high-risk lender of last resort.
- Underworld medicine. [1779]-[1781]: criminals need discreet physicians; no separate underworld school.
- Scale by place (no numbers anywhere). Cairnholt: politically sensitive, access/information/corruption ([1784]). Toll's Reach: broadest visible overlap, dozens of specialist networks, no superstructure ([1787]-[1788]). Aldergate: money and paperwork, not theatrical street gangs ([1790]). Waymeet: transient, mobility defines it ([1793]). Windmere: "largest and most international underworld outside the Hearth" ([1806]). Greywood: vice under respectable fronts ([1811]). Sunreach: regulatory arbitrage ([1815]). Thornward: roads, garrisons, banditry ([1819]). Stormhollow: labour, ore, debt ([1823]). Dunmoor: land, horses, patrons ([1802]). Independent belts: no shared culture ([1827]).
- Categories. Outlaw, criminal, rebel, bandit and royal agent are separate; do not flatten all non-state violence to "bandit" ([1833]-[1834]).
- Simulation principles. [1837] track ownership, debts, contracts, affiliations, trusted physicians, blacklists, fighter records, injuries, known and hidden corruption, membership, reputation, secret identity, fronts, protection, cross-border contacts; do not reduce crime to one meter. [4413]-[4414] separate truth from belief. [6052] a later registry pass may seed senior figures asymmetrically. [1839] vice stays human in scale.

## 5. Gaps and contradictions

Canon items with no (or null) engine presence
- `underworld_institution_profiles` is empty: no named canon institution carries engine operating-model data.
- No canon criminal organisation is in `underworld_organizations`. The Aldergate underworld is wholly procedural (10 invented gangs plus Reese Exchange) while canon names the Tallow Family as the Aldergate dynasty and says street gangs rise and vanish while durable power owns property and fronts ([1738]).
- Tallow, Greyhook and Saltwake are `retired` quarry actors (v0.60 rebuild), contradicting canon's WORKING-and-active status ([1840], [5909]-[5927]).
- Red Board has no location, no branches (Cairnholt, Toll's Reach) and no members; Mallow has no location or members; Dunmoor Bridle Houses and Stonebridge Escorts have no rows.
- Engine zones and organisations exist only for Aldergate; canon underworld settings for Toll's Reach, Cairnholt, Saltmere, Crownhold, Waymeet, Lysmere, Aldenmoor, Ravenfield, Stormhollow, Brackenfell have no engine representation.
- Canon places without location rows: Greyhook wharf, Toll's Reach warehouse districts, Aldergate caravan quarter and canon-sourced ward names (engine's Aldergate ward names are procedural).
- Roles with no actor category: access brokers, fences, predatory lenders, bookmakers, pit promoters (only racket kinds exist: unlicensed bookmaking, protection, invoice fraud, brothel protection, courtesan-house leverage, etc.).

Engine items contradicting or straining canon
