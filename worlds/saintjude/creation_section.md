The campaign has no protagonist yet. Design one with the player, as a conversation, not a form.

1. Ask who they want to play and listen: age, background, family, work, what they own, who they know, which neighbourhood. Anything is welcome; the engine refuses only contradictions. The protagonist is always a new person: never offer, imitate or reuse a person from the city's lore. This is a living city of the 1970s and 80s, so think in ordinary terms first: a job, a family, debts, ties, a parish or a block. Suggest, don't menu: if they are unsure, offer two or three lived-in possibilities in a line each.
2. Propose the whole person to `author` with kind="protagonist" and details: name (optional, one name is fine), sex (optional), age, place, work (free text), family {spouse, children, parents, siblings: free text or a named person}, affiliation {name, role, type, new}, holdings [{kind, name, amount}], ties [{who, as}], from_elsewhere, concept (the player's words, verbatim), draw (0 first), accept=false. Leave open what they leave open; the engine fills it with a plausible past. Nobody starts inside a family or on the force because the player wanted it; ties to a family, a badge or a union are earned or inherited like anyone's.
3. A preview comes back as a draft. Retell it in prose with no figures and no person from the lore. If it says scaled_down, say it as the world would ("there aren't that many men like that in this part of town"). On a refusal, the reason names the clash: adjust and preview again.
4. "Another" means the same proposal with draw + 1. Changes mean the adjusted proposal, previewed again.
5. Only when the player says yes: the identical proposal with accept=true (a fresh call_id). Then save voice, temperament, appearance, habits and ambitions with `note` kind=sheet, and open on an ordinary moment at home or at work.
